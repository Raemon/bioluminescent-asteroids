# Pilot's Log — Entry Three

**Triggered at:** combo x12 (the next milestone above x6 / Entry 1).
**Register:** half-prose, half-murmur. Entry 2 was the lullaby high-water mark; Entry 3 starts the contraction. Captain is more tired, less coaxing the kid toward sleep, more talking past her to himself. Internal rhyme is the new texture — the captain's mouth slipping into faint sing-song *inside* lines without committing to it at the line-end. No end-of-line rhyme. No predictable structure.
**Voice:** same character (PL_Ralf_Deep, pitched-down, scratchy-radio FX bed). Slower than Entry 2. Drier. He's not performing for the kid anymore; he's leaving the mic on while he thinks out loud.

**Design-doc guardrail:** the cryptic-signal-from-the-dark thread is for **later** entries ("don't blow it early. The captain knows; he just hasn't said"). Entry 3 stays grounded in physical detail — the kind of mundane thing a real pilot logs. A numb knuckle at hour four. Coffee gone since Tuesday. The mystery stays in the *form* (lullaby fighting the content), not the content.

> *Bent it loose against the console. Hurts in a way I'd forgotten about.*

>
> *...You should be asleep. I'm fine out here. Go on.*

---

## Why these words — Variant A

The brief: **internal rhyme, no end-of-line rhyme, irregular placement.** Every rhyme below lives somewhere *inside* a line. None at a phrase-end. The ear should feel something tugging the line into faint cadence without ever being able to point at where the rhyme is.

- **"Hey, kid."** — the form. He always opens this way; he doesn't even hear himself doing it.
- **"Long one tonight. Hands went cold around hour four — you know that knuckle that locks up? Locked."** — the load-bearing concrete detail of the entry. Astronaut logs are full of body complaints; pilots more so. Internal rhymes:
  - **hour four** — the one clean internal rhyme. Two stressed monosyllables sharing a vowel, dropped mid-line, never at a phrase end. This is the "more actual rhyme" the brief asks for — audible but never closing. Coach the actor to deliver it as two facts of life, not a rhyme.
  - **knuckle / locks up** — slant /k/-/k/ consonant echo, mid-line.
  - **locks up? Locked** — the period-then-repeat is the captain naming the thing twice because he's mad at it. Reads as prose, lands percussive on the beat.
- **"Bent it loose against the console. Hurts in a way I'd forgotten about."** — *Hurts in a way I'd forgotten about* — slant /-uh-/ across *Hurts / forgotten / about*, three mid-line beats with shared vowel shape. Faint cadence, no closure.
- **"Coffee's been gone since Tuesday. I miscounted the packets."** — the coffee callback from the design conversation (Entry 1 draft: *"tell Maggie the coffee's gone — she'll laugh"*). The joke landed; now the joke's gone bad and the captain doesn't know it. *Tuesday / miscounted* — vowel slant mid-line.
- **"Tell your mother that one. She'll laugh."** — Maggie returns, named the way the design doc allows (*your mother*, not *Maggie*, addressed via the kid). *That one* points back at the coffee detail — the captain is consciously selecting a line he thinks Maggie will find funny, which means he's not telling her the lines he *won't*. The structural reveal is in what he chooses to send home.
- **"...You should be asleep. I'm fine out here. Go on."** — the polite lie. Two internal rhymes:
  - **asleep / fine** — slant /i/ across the line.
  - **out here / Go on** — slant — *here / Go* share nothing on paper, but both stressed monosyllables open the vowel back to /-eh-/-/-oh-/; the line tugs against itself.

## The line — Variant B (more deliberate internal rhyme, audible without landing)

> *Hey, kid.*
> *Long shift. Cold start, slow heart, knuckle locked around hour four.*
> *Bent it loose on the console. Stings now in a way I half forgot.*
>
> *Coffee's been gone since Tuesday. Miscounted the packets — pack of fool, this old man.*
> *Tell your mother. She'll laugh and she'll know.*
>
> *...You should be down. I'm fine up here. Go on, now. Go on.*

---

## Why this version — Variant B

The rhymes are closer to the surface. You can hear them on first listen. They still stay *internal* (mid-line) and they still avoid clean line-end resolution. The captain is a little further gone here than in Variant A — more sing-song leaking through, less guard on the mic.

- **"Long shift. Cold start, slow heart, knuckle locked around hour four."** — three deliberate internal rhymes in one line:
  - **start / heart** — clean monosyllabic vowel rhyme, mid-line, comma-separated. The line a working pilot might actually say (cold-start = engine; slow heart = tired body; both true at once). Plausibly deniable as prose.
  - **hour four** — same as Variant A but now sitting *after* two other rhymes in the same line, so the density is the move.
  - **knuckle / locked** — slant /k/-/k/, mid-line.
- **"Bent it loose on the console. Stings now in a way I half forgot."** — *Stings now / half forgot* — slant /-ow/-/-aw/, mid-line and end. No clean closure.
- **"Coffee's been gone since Tuesday. Miscounted the packets — pack of fool, this old man."** — the line lets a colloquialism in: *pack of fool* is the kind of self-deprecating mutter a tired man makes when he realizes he ran out of something. Three internal rhymes:
  - **Tuesday / Miscounted** — vowel slant.
  - **packets / pack of** — anaphora-into-pun. *Pack of fool* is the captain looking at the empty wrapper and calling himself a *pack of fool* — the kind of accidental word salad tired people make.
  - **fool / old** — slant /uh/ vowel mid-line.
- **"Tell your mother. She'll laugh and she'll know."** — *laugh / know* slant; *and / and* doesn't appear but the rhythm is anaphora-adjacent. *She'll know* opens a small gap — what will she know? Not specified. That's the entry's quiet gut-punch.
- **"...You should be down. I'm fine up here. Go on, now. Go on."** — *down / now* — clean monosyllabic mid-line vowel rhyme. *Go on, now. Go on.* — repetition. The captain says it twice because the kid isn't there to say "okay" back the first time.

### Variant B direction note

Because the rhymes are more audible, the *delivery* needs to compensate — even drier, more murmured, more conversational than Variant A. If the actor leans into "cold start, slow heart" as a rhyme, the take tips over into pastiche. Coach toward "tired guy describing a malfunction to no one in particular." The rhymes should feel like accidents his mouth made.

---

## Direction for the take

- **Mic distance:** same as Entry 2 (very close), even less affect. He's leaving the PTT open while he thinks.
- **Tempo:** slower than Entry 1, similar to or slightly slower than Entry 2. Plenty of breath between phrases. The fraying shows up in **how much silence he leaves between thoughts**, not in shorter sentences per se.
- **Pitch:** stay flat. Entry 2 allowed melodic contour on "low, lower, home" — *don't* do that here. Entry 3 is past lullaby. Talking, not almost-singing.
- **No tenderness.** Entry 2 was the captain leaning over a child. Entry 3 is the captain leaving the mic open while he stares at a gauge. The kid is the listener but not the audience.
- **"hour four" is the load-bearing internal rhyme** in Variant A; **"start / heart" + "down / now"** in Variant B. They should land *without being delivered as rhymes*. Two separate facts the actor happens to say in the same line.
- **Avoid:** any sing-song delivery. Both lines flirt with cadence and both fail-state if the actor sings them. Coach toward the kind of "go to sleep" a dad mutters into the next room without turning around.
