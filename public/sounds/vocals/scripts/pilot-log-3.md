# Pilot's Log — Entry Three

**Triggered at:** combo x12 (the next milestone above x6 / Entry 1, and above Entry 2's provisional x10).
**Register:** half-prose, half-murmur. Entry 2 was the lullaby high-water mark; Entry 3 starts the contraction. The captain is more tired, less coaxing the kid toward sleep, more talking past the kid to himself. Internal rhyme is the new texture — the captain's mouth slipping into faint sing-song *inside* lines without committing to it at the line-end. No end-of-line rhyme. No predictable structure.
**Voice:** same character (PL_Ralf_Deep, pitched-down, scratchy-radio FX bed). Slower than Entry 2. Drier. He's not performing for the kid anymore; he's leaving the mic on while he thinks out loud.

---

## The line — Variant A (internal rhyme, subtler)

> *Hey, kid.*
> *Relay's been clicking. Quiet, steady — every other minute, regular as breath.*
> *Almost answered the thing. Almost said go ahead.*
> *Couldn't tell you what comes after that.*
>
> *Coffee's gone. Held the gauge through the long shift.*
> *Tell your mother — she'll know what that means.*
>
> *...Go on. Hush a while. I'm still here.*

---

## Why these words — Variant A

The brief: **internal rhyme, no end-of-line rhyme, irregular placement, no predictable structure.** Every rhyme below lives somewhere *inside* a line. None of them land at a phrase-end. The ear should feel something tugging the line into faint cadence without ever being able to point at where the rhyme is.

- **"Hey, kid."** — same hook as Entry 1 and Entry 2. It's the *form* of the log now. He always opens this way; he doesn't even hear himself doing it.
- **"Relay's been clicking. Quiet, steady — every other minute, regular as breath."** — picks a single concrete detail (per the design brief) the way real astronauts log: an instrument doing something it shouldn't quite be doing. Internal rhymes inside the line:
  - **clicking / steady** — slant vowel-shape echo (*ih / eh*) and shared soft consonant tail, mid-line.
  - **every other minute, regular as breath** — *every / regular* share the schwa-e mid-line; *minute / regular* share an /r/-echo. None of them at a line end.
  - The line *reads* as plain prose. The faint cadence underneath is the trick.
- **"Almost answered the thing. Almost said go ahead."** — anaphora (*Almost… Almost…*) reads as lullaby-form and as tired-adult-repeating-himself at once. The clean internal rhyme tucked here is **answered / said** — both short-A-then-soft-D, mid-line. Read it aloud: the ear catches a couplet-feel without the line ever closing one.
- **"Couldn't tell you what comes after that."** — flat ending. No rhyme. The line that *should* close the stanza (in song form) refuses to. That refusal is the entry's structural move: the captain isn't completing the thought.
- **"Coffee's gone. Held the gauge through the long shift."** — the deliberate rhyme of the whole take: **gone / long**, a clean monosyllabic vowel rhyme dropped *mid-line, mid-clause*. The brief asked for more actual rhyme than Entry 2 — this is it. It's clean enough that the ear catches it, but it's nowhere near a line-end, so it never reads as a couplet. Coffee detail is the throughline from the design conversation ("tell Maggie the coffee's gone — she'll laugh"); now the coffee is gone for real and there's no joke.
- **"Tell your mother — she'll know what that means."** — Maggie returns, unnamed, the way she should be (the design brief calls *Maggie* the only other allowed name; using *your mother* keeps the kid-addressed frame intact and lets the listener place Maggie if they remember her from Entry 1's drafts). The line is the entry's quiet gut-punch: he's encoding something to his wife through their child that he doesn't want the kid to understand. *that means* echoes *gauge / shift* through a soft -ng/-ft/-ns tail — felt, not heard.
- **"...Go on. Hush a while. I'm still here."** — the polite lie. Two internal rhymes do double duty:
  - **Hush / still** — slant (*uh / ih*) — mid-line, irregularly placed.
  - **while / I'm / fine** — actually no, the line ends *"still here"*, not *"fine"*. The internal cluster is **a while / I'm** — short-i vowel echo across the comma. The line lands on *still here*, which is the lie. He won't be.

## The line — Variant B (more deliberate internal rhyme, audible without landing)

> *Hey, kid.*
> *Relay's tapping, quiet as a cat's foot — every other minute, keeping time on me.*
> *Almost answered it. Almost said hello, friend, what's the trouble.*
> *Couldn't tell you what I'd've followed that with.*
>
> *Coffee's gone. Gauge held all night — tell your mother. The long watch is the kind she'd know.*
>
> *...Go on now. Lie still. I'm fine.*

---

## Why this version — Variant B

The rhymes are closer to the surface. You can hear them on first listen. They still stay *internal* (mid-line) and they still avoid clean line-end resolution. The captain is a little further gone here than in Variant A — more sing-song leaking through, less guard on the mic.

- **"tapping, quiet as a cat's foot"** — *tapping / cat's* is the first deliberate internal rhyme, mid-line, short-A vowel shared across two stressed syllables. "Quiet as a cat's foot" is also a real idiom a working-class American might use — keeps it deniable as prose.
- **"every other minute, keeping time on me"** — *every / keeping* slant (vowel-e mid-line); *minute / time* slant (-i- mid-line); both faint. **"keeping time on me"** is also the diegetic-frame collapse this entry's version of Entry 2's "low, lower, home": the captain is naming the rhythm of the game — the pad's downbeat — as something the *relay* is doing to him.
- **"Almost answered it. Almost said hello, friend, what's the trouble."** — anaphora again. The cluster **friend / trouble** is the entry's audible-but-not-landing internal rhyme: same nasal-to-bilabial closure (-nd / -bl), no clean vowel match. Reads as something a worn-out man would say without meaning to rhyme.
- **"Couldn't tell you what I'd've followed that with."** — refusal to close, same as Variant A. The flat tail is doing the same structural work.
- **"Coffee's gone. Gauge held all night — tell your mother. The long watch is the kind she'd know."** — three internal rhymes, none at a line-end:
  - **gone / long** — same clean vowel rhyme as Variant A. The deliberate one.
  - **night / kind / know** — alliterative /-n/ + /-aɪ/ cluster, mid-line.
  - **long watch / she'd know** — *watch / know* is consonant-soft slant (-tch / -oh), tucked inside.
  - The line is denser than Variant A's equivalent — that's the version's whole bet: more rhyme audible, still no closure.
- **"...Go on now. Lie still. I'm fine."** — Variant B's polite lie is *I'm fine* instead of *I'm still here*. The clean internal rhyme is **lie / fine** — short-/i/ vowel rhyme inside the line. "Lie / fine" is also a quiet pun on the lie itself — but only if the listener catches it, and the captain wouldn't.

### Variant B direction note

Because the rhymes are slightly more audible, the *delivery* needs to compensate — even drier, more murmured, more conversational than Variant A. If the actor leans into "tapping / cat's" or "friend / trouble" as rhymes, the take tips over into pastiche. Coach toward "tired guy describing a malfunction to no one in particular." The rhymes should feel like accidents his mouth made.

---

## Direction for the take

- **Mic distance:** same as Entry 2 (very close) but with even less affect. He's leaving the PTT open while he thinks. The radio compression is doing most of the work.
- **Tempo:** slower than Entry 1, similar to or slightly slower than Entry 2. Plenty of breath between phrases. The fraying shows up in **how much silence the captain leaves between thoughts**, not in shorter sentences per se.
- **Pitch:** stay flat. The melodic contour Entry 2 allowed on "low, lower, home" — *don't* do that here. Entry 3 is past lullaby. Talking, not almost-singing.
- **No tenderness.** Entry 2 was the captain leaning over a child. Entry 3 is the captain leaving the mic open while he stares at a gauge. The kid is the listener but not the audience.
- **The "gone / long" internal rhyme is the load-bearing one.** It should land *without being delivered as a rhyme*. Treat it as two separate clauses the actor happens to say in the same line. If the actor pauses on "gone" and pauses on "long," the rhyme reveals itself; if the actor leans into either word, the trick collapses.
- **Avoid:** any sing-song delivery on "Hush a while" / "Lie still." Both lines flirt with lullaby cadence and both fail-state if the actor sings them. Coach toward the kind of "go to sleep" a dad mutters into the next room without turning around.
