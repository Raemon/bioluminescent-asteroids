# Pilot's Log — Entry Two

**Triggered at:** (TBD — next combo milestone above 6; provisional 10x or 12x)
**Register:** half-prose, half-lullaby, half-rap. The captain is murmur-singing to a kid who isn't there. Slant rhymes only — nothing that lands square. If a take feels like it scans, soften the rhyme until it almost doesn't.
**Voice:** same character as Entry 1 (Bastion-narrator-adjacent, pitched-down 1 semitone, scratchy-radio FX bed). Slightly more tired than Entry 1. Lower volume on the mic, like he's leaned closer.

## The line

> *Hey, kid.*
> *Couldn't sleep, huh.*
> *Me neither — funny how that works.*
>
> *Listen. Close your eyes.*
> *There's three notes out here.*
> *They go… low, lower, home.*
> *Same three, every time. Counted 'em.*
>
> *That's the trick, see.*
> *You don't count sheep out here.*
> *You count the ones that sing back.*
>
> *...One for the dark.*
> *One for the long way.*
> *One for the porch light.*
>
> *Go on. I got the watch.*

---

## Why these words

- **"Hey, kid. / Couldn't sleep, huh."** — opens with the same hook as Entry 1 ("Hey, kid"), establishes it as a *form*. The "huh" answers a question the kid didn't ask, which tells you the captain is filling in both sides of the conversation now. Lullaby register established immediately.
- **"funny how that works"** — throwaway, but it's the kind of thing a tired adult mutters to a child to make insomnia feel like a shared joke instead of a problem. Off-rhyme: *huh / works* (consonant echo only).
- **"three notes out here / low, lower, home"** — picks up the unused thread from Claude's earlier draft. The pad melody in the game has a literal G–F–G–C lift. "Low, lower, home" is the captain *describing the actual game music*, which is the same trick Entry 1 ran with "it's loud." The player is hearing what the captain is hearing.
- **"low, lower, home"** is the rap spine of the entry — three stressed syllables, the second is a near-repeat of the first (off-rhyme by intensification), the third is the resolution. Sing-songable; deniable as prose.
- **"Counted 'em."** — clipped, percussive. Lands hard on the beat. Breath catch after.
- **"You don't count sheep out here. / You count the ones that sing back."** — the lullaby trope flipped into the diegetic frame. *sheep / sing back* is a soft slant — sibilant echo, no real rhyme. This is the entry's emotional move: the captain has been alone long enough that the sky has started keeping him company, and he's trying to explain that to the kid as if it's normal.
- **"One for the dark. / One for the long way. / One for the porch light."** — the lullaby-counting beat. Three matched cadences, each shorter than a real sentence. Off-rhymes are *dark / way / light* — a deliberately broken set; if it rhymed cleanly it would read as nursery rhyme, but the mismatch keeps it on the side of half-remembered murmur. The "porch light" is the only image that points back to Earth — the only thing he names that isn't out here with him. That's the entry's quiet gut-punch.
- **"Go on. I got the watch."** — the closer. Echoes "I'll be home soon" from Entry 1 as a structural *polite lie* — the captain reassures the kid in language the kid would recognize from a parent staying up to watch over them, but "the watch" is the *bridge watch*, the long shift, what he is actually doing and what is actually keeping him from sleep. Same line doing both jobs. Off-rhyme: *on / watch* (vowel echo, no resolution).

## Variant B — rhymes a touch more deliberately

Same character, same beat grid, same lullaby-fighting-the-content register. The rhymes are closer to the surface — you can hear them on first listen — but they're still slant, still uneven, still landing on consonants and vowel-shapes rather than clean A/A endings. Think Tom Waits muttering to himself, not nursery rhyme. The captain isn't trying to be clever; he's just slipped a little further into sing-song because it's late and he's been talking to no one for a long time.

> *Hey, kid.*
> *Couldn't sleep again.*
> *Yeah. Me too. Been a while since I did.*
>
> *Listen. Eyes closed.*
> *There's three notes the dark keeps.*
> *Low. Lower. Home.*
> *Same three, every night I don't sleep.*
>
> *That's the trick of it, see.*
> *You don't count sheep out this far.*
> *You count the ones that hum the bar.*
>
> *...One for the long dark.*
> *One for the long road.*
> *One for the porch we left on.*
>
> *Go on now. Eyes shut.*
> *I got the watch. I got the hold.*

### Why this version

- **"sleep again / did"** — soft slant, vowel echo only. Sets up that this version is going to flirt with rhyme without committing.
- **"keeps / sleep"** — the first deliberately-audible rhyme, but separated by two lines so the ear barely catches it. He's not landing it on purpose; it just fell that way.
- **"see / far / bar"** — *far/bar* is the most deliberate rhyme in the whole take. Lands clean. This is the moment the captain accidentally raps a bar at himself and almost notices. Coach the actor to deliver "bar" as a sigh, not a punchline — the rhyme should feel like something his mouth did without him.
- **"long dark / long road / porch we left on"** — the lullaby triplet. *dark / road* is a soft consonant rhyme (hard-stop endings); *left on* breaks the pattern intentionally so the third line lands with weight instead of closure. Same structural move as Variant A's broken triplet, but tighter.
- **"shut / watch / hold"** — closing triplet of single-syllable hard endings, all near each other in vowel shape (*uh / ah / oh*). Doesn't rhyme. Echoes. The "hold" is new — *holding the line, holding the kid, holding the ship together* — and rhyme-adjacent to "road" two lines back, which gives the take a faint A/B/A spine without ever stating it.
- **The added "long" repetition** (*long dark / long road*) is the version's main concession to song-form. Anaphora reads as lullaby; reads as prose in the captain's mouth because tired people repeat words. Both jobs.
- **Direction note specific to Variant B:** because the rhymes are slightly more audible, the *delivery* needs to compensate by being even more murmured and conversational than Variant A. If the take leans into the rhymes vocally, this version tips over into pastiche. The rhymes have to feel discovered, not performed.

## Direction for the take

- **Mic distance:** closer than Entry 1. He's whispering it, not transmitting it. The radio compression should be doing more work than the lungs.
- **Tempo:** slower than Entry 1. Lullaby tempo. Let the silences breathe.
- **Pitch:** he's *not* singing. But "low, lower, home" and the three "One for the…" lines should sit slightly into a melodic contour — falling, falling, slight lift. As if he's about to sing and stops himself.
- **No smile in the voice.** Tired adult, not tender adult. The tenderness has to be in the *form* (lullaby) fighting the *content* (a man alone on watch). If the voice itself sounds tender, the whole thing tips sentimental.
- **Avoid:** any clean rhyme delivery. If the actor leans into "sheep / sing back" as a rhyme it ruins the whole register. Coach toward prose-reading with a faint cadence underneath.
